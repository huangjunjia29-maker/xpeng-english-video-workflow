# XPENG Daily Workflow Specification

## Daily Intake

Confirm only information that has not already been decided: copy count, direction count and themes, hashtags, canvas, authorized source boundary, mandatory assets and order, total-coverage requirements, and prohibited media. The daily copy count is never assumed. Four communication directions are the default, with small adjustments when the brief needs them.

Before each creation, ask which video form to use (source clips, image-led edits, narrated B-roll, or an approved mixture), whether narration is wanted, and whether footage-source labels should appear on screen. Do not default to English creator narration or mandatory source labels. If narration is selected, confirm language and voice/style as needed. Reuse explicit answers for the current batch; historical batch choices do not replace this check. Keep an internal source map regardless of visible labels, and never present historical or concept footage as evidence of a current test.

Treat user instructions as operations and attached documents as facts. Extract the full document text, tables, hyperlinks, embedded images, and relevant metadata. For cloud pages, scroll through lazy-loaded content and recount assets.

When a cloud page or media node returns an access error, save the URL and exact status in the batch source record. Do not treat the URL, thumbnail, or document reference as a downloaded original. Continue with local-library material only when the user authorized that fallback for the batch, and keep the fallback matched to the same model, event, market, period, and evidence role. Never reuse an older single-period poster as proof of a newer or multi-period result. If a current official poster is unavailable, either omit it or use an explicitly editorial fact card based on the authorized brief; record that the card is not the official artwork and cite the brief's source.

Every newly supplied file, folder, and accessible cloud/source asset must be archived during intake to `D:/视频素材库`, classified by content and deduplicated against the entire library by SHA-256. Keep source files intact, index existing identical files rather than copying them again, and report any unavailable originals or inaccessible links. Follow `asset-library.md`; complete and verify archiving before calling the batch finished. On a device without this drive, report the missing destination and ask for its replacement rather than claiming success.

## Communication Directions and Copy

Directions must differ in substance. Useful angles include a concrete user scene, the practical meaning of a technical metric, third-party or market evidence, and the larger company or product roadmap. Confirm directions before writing the complete batch.

When the user adopts a creator brief, make its selected angles, intended audience, evidence, and hooks the basis of both copy and video plans. Treat the creator perspective as a writing approach, not a compulsory voiceover format. Present directions as `Direction N: Title (Focus on ...)` followed by an actionable English paragraph about how a KOC can use the available evidence; add Chinese counterparts when requested.

Write bilingual KOC-style posts. Each post should have one main idea and an independent opening hook. Avoid official `we/our` voice, press-release cadence, unsupported superlatives, competitor attacks, and Chinese-to-English translation syntax.

For AI and driving claims, distinguish:

- capabilities shown or deployed in the authorized source;
- performance figures reported by XPENG;
- announced or planned vehicle rollout;
- long-term roadmap items such as Robotaxi, robotics, or flying cars.

Do not turn a demo into a universal safety claim. Preserve time, version, market, model, test condition, and source qualifiers where relevant.

For market rankings and sales results, treat the full scope as one indivisible fact: reporting period, market, product segment, metric, and cited source. Keep that scope beside `No.1`, `best-selling`, or similar wording in copy and factual on-screen cards. Do not generalize a monthly regional segment ranking to all MPVs, all EVs, global leadership, cumulative leadership, or repeat purchase behavior. A previous period's poster cannot substitute for the current claim even when the model and market match.

Keep copy continuously numbered and follow the batch's requested order. Group by direction for review when requested; shuffle only when requested. A video-matched delivery always follows the actual agreed video-file order and must not be reshuffled afterward. Validate exact count, direction allocation, bilingual completeness, uniqueness, hashtags, qualifiers, and character limits. Unless a platform rule says otherwise, English copy including hashtags should remain within 200 characters. Export only one `Copy` sheet with `No.`, `English`, and `中文`; add `视频文件名` when matching posts to videos, keeping English in column B for easy pasting.

## Video Planning and Rendering

Recommend 5-20 master videos based on natural chapters, source quality, evidence coverage, and repetition. Do not fix the count at nine or require three per direction. Explain the proposed count and direction allocation for each batch, then confirm the plan unless already approved. Do not invent scenes, split incomplete speech, or pad footage to reach the range; explain when available material cannot support it.

Use the brief's hooks for approved top titles and openings. If a direction has more masters than supplied hooks, write additional hooks consistent with that direction, tone, evidence, and claim limits. Do not invent capabilities or promises. Record each master's direction, hook, sources, and optional narration in the working plan. See `master-expansion-and-copy-matching.md` for plugin expansion and ordered copy pairing.

Use at least five images per image-led video when enough authorized material exists. Keep each image visible for about three seconds; transitions are part of that time.

For story-led creation, translate each adopted brief angle and its proposed shooting method into a scene sequence: a concrete opening question or reaction, progressively matched visual evidence, then a payoff or closing question that answers the opening. Inspect an authorized reference for this narrative structure rather than copying unrelated claims or media. Validate one complete sample before expanding a new format. Distinguish masters through story progression, evidence, opening and ending, and coherent typography; changing image order alone is insufficient.

For narrated scenes, actual speech duration and natural release take precedence over the approximate three-second image slot. Keep photographs static when the user asks for stable images: disable shake, simulated handheld movement, and push/pull zoom. Use restrained cuts or fades where they support the story; this does not prohibit transitions in every future batch. See `voiceover-broll-mode.md` for voice, scene timing, and separate BGM delivery.

For future creative edits, favor richer, more expansive compositions when the source supports them. Select high-resolution portrait photographs that can fill the vertical canvas while preserving faces, the vehicle, and meaningful surroundings. Native portrait media should feel immersive rather than being reduced to a small central inset. Keep landscape media complete at canvas width over full-canvas blur when needed; do not force a landscape source into portrait by stretching or cutting away important content.

Build visual variety into the shot plan: people and interaction, a clean vehicle exterior or wider setting, then cabin or design details as the story calls for them. Weave additional same-model beauty photographs into natural openings, breathing spaces, scene bridges, or the closing payoff when reuse is authorized. They should contribute to the subject, not be confined automatically to one brief final insert or added as filler. Preserve the source/version distinction and evidence shots; a beauty photograph cannot prove a spoken event or feature claim. This preference does not authorize unrelated library assets or changes to a locked user edit.

Create a more polished, spacious feel through strong full-frame images, a clear focal subject, balanced negative space, restrained text, and consistent color treatment. Vary framing and scene scale across the sequence instead of repeatedly using similar group photos or identical blurred-inset layouts. Keep photo motion stable as requested, and use cuts or restrained transitions to support narrative rhythm. Do not substitute extra effects, constant zooming, or faster cutting for stronger imagery. Review a sequence contact sheet alongside playback for redundant shots, image quality, full-screen portrait coverage, title safety, and continuity before batch rendering; no fixed portrait or beauty-shot quota is required.

When approved library footage supplements a current batch, distinguish evidence from illustration in the source map. Market-specific street footage may support place and context, while country-unspecified product or family imagery is illustrative only. Do not imply that an illustrated feature is available in the named market, that staged people are actual buyers, or that lifestyle footage proves why a ranking occurred.

For source-video clips, inspect the actual audiovisual sequence around every proposed boundary. A cut is valid only when the current speaker has finished the sentence or self-contained thought, or when the source itself makes a clear editorial transition. Watch the speaker, listen through the line, and read any burned-in subtitle; scene detection, silence detection, transcripts, and contact sheets are supporting tools rather than sufficient evidence. Never divide the source into equal-duration sections merely to hit a target count. Allow unequal durations, fewer clips, or limited overlap when that preserves complete speech and coherent context.

Preserve the intake of breath before a line and the natural release after its final syllable. A subtitle disappearing or an ASR word ending does not establish a safe cut. Prefer an adjacent complete B-roll shot to end a clip when the interview otherwise feels abruptly stopped; do not import a fragment of the previous or next speaker. Avoid fades or audio crossfades over speech and breathing. Choose these margins from the actual performance, not a fixed padding duration.

An end-card picture and its underlying audio need not share a cut point. If removing the card would truncate the final response, preserve the relevant original audio and cover the unwanted picture with appropriate moving footage from the authorized event. Keep lip sync during visible speech, avoid unrelated burned-in captions or a misleading reaction, and inspect the resulting sound transition. Do not invent missing speech, freeze or loop a frame to reach a duration, or retain an unwanted logo sting without checking it.

If the user supplies clips they have already edited and asks only for vertical/horizontal conversion, resolution conversion, or another narrow transformation, preserve their edit decisions exactly. Do not change in/out points, internal timing, shot order, audio timing, subtitles, or content unless the user explicitly requests it. For horizontal media converted to vertical, keep the full horizontal frame centered at canvas width over a canvas-filling blurred background unless the user specifies another layout; native vertical media stays full-frame. Avoid empty side borders and stretching.

Output vertical `720x1280` or horizontal `1280x720`, 30fps, with normal pixel aspect ratio and at least 12 seconds per video. Preserve source audio and burned-in subtitles unless the user changes that requirement. Do not add music, titles, logos, or end cards without authorization.

## QA and Delivery

Check every final video through full decode and representative frames at the beginning, middle, end, transition points, user-reported timestamps, and source boundaries. For speech-led clips, listen across both sides of every boundary and confirm no spoken sentence, subtitle phrase, gesture, or response is cut off. For user-edited locked clips, compare input and output duration and audio timing to confirm the requested conversion did not alter the edit. Verify resolution, frame rate, duration, audio, subtitle baseline, title safe area, required asset order, batch coverage, black frames, stretching, clipping, jump frames, logos, and watermarks.

Deliver only final approved files. Store scripts, intermediate renders, original mechanical subtitles, contact sheets, and logs outside the delivery directory.

Record what was actually reviewed. Waveform comparisons can verify preserved audio intervals but cannot certify natural breathing or listening quality. If continuous audiovisual playback is unavailable, continue the authorized checks and provide a clearly identified review version with listening QA pending; do not report that it was heard or fully approved. For revised deliverables, use an explicit revision filename and update the landing page so a cached old video is not mistaken for the replacement. Keep the prior render recoverable outside the current delivery set.

A browser's playing or unmuted state, screenshots, burned-in captions, and ASR text do not establish that its audio was heard. When a reference is accessible only visually, explain that limitation and identify which structure or caption evidence informed the edit. Do not claim audio quality or naturalness was verified through transcription alone.

When matching expanded videos to copy, verify every video's direction, one-to-one file coverage, unique copy use, and Excel row order. The copy must fit that video's actual content as well as its direction; it is not necessarily a narration transcript. Deliver a short verification summary and stop before publishing unless separately authorized.
